# 2026-04-25 — Strategic Board Session: Convergence Memo

**Format:** four-round convene of the strategic-tier advisory board (Branson / Munger / Singer / Naval / Greene / Godin), context-naive.
**Trigger:** felt heaviness and stiffness in the transition; plan looked coherent on paper but something underneath wasn't moving.
**Outcome:** the engagement-shapes page (`/work-with-me`) shipped on boringsystems, plus calibration fixes to the advisor architecture.

This memo is the *succinct* artifact of the session — what converged, what diverged, what was decided, what to revisit.

---

## What converged across all four rounds

**The work is already happening.** Five of six advisors, every round, said the same thing: the 3–6 in-person conversations per week + the five named people served this week + the two published Building-lane pieces (*The Operator's AI Stack* and *The Agent Harness That Runs 80% of My Work*) + the live demo that produced a downstream car-wash-agency build = the practice. Not preparation. Naval was sharpest: *"three weeks into the actual game and you don't see it because you're still measuring against the old one."*

**The load-bearing block is "shadow until success."** Unanimous (6/6, every round). The belief that the transition has to stay invisible until it has succeeded is the closed loop — because the success criterion requires the visibility being avoided. Munger called it status-management dressed as patience. Greene noted Buffett/Munger were shadowed in *output*, not in *relationships* — known intimately by twenty people, which is exactly the current state.

**Hold the four entry-points open.** 5/6 said do not collapse them prematurely. Let market evidence (Naval) or weight (Greene) collapse them. Naval said let one die by day 45. *"You're not choosing a path. You're subtracting until one is obvious. 30 months of runway is 30 months of permission to let things die."* Branson alone wanted slight collapse — *front door + rooms*.

**On the page (final round).** 5/6 said build it as a *permission artifact* — sent when asked, never broadcast. Specs converged tightly: four shapes, one paragraph each, harness-piece voice, no prices, no CTA, no testimonials, no logos, no SEO, no "I help X do Y", top line + email + that's it. Singer alone said don't build it — the document is the resistance.

**On charging.** Bar reframed unanimously by 4/6 in the final round: not "someone paying me" but *"someone paid me **for the pattern**, and I know **which shape** they bought."* The car-wash demo example almost literally fits Naval's variants (cash / equity / rev-share / build-and-hand-off). 90-day target: one paid engagement of any shape, any size — even small.

**Posture.** Five different sentences for the same posture: *bill, don't scale. Practice-form, not company-form.* Branson: "business, converting deliberately." Munger: "hobbyist that converts on pull." Naval: "practitioner publishing in public, quiet door open." Greene: "business at hobbyist cadence — practice that bills, not company that hires." Godin: "smallest viable business — one paying person per shape." Singer alone: "neither — life will answer the income question, not you."

**Carry — said six different ways, all the same:** the work is already inviting itself into the world; the architect-mind's job is to stop interrupting it.

## What diverged (worth keeping in view)

**Singer's frame-refusal.** In every round, Singer dissented from the strategic frame itself. *"Stop preparing for the work and let the work find you — it already is."* The dissent isn't a contradiction — it's a calibration on whether to orient toward conversion at all. Hold it as the floor. The five who say "be ready when it asks for money" are not wrong. Singer is the one who says even the readiness can become noise.

**Pricing on the page.** Branson alone wanted explicit pricing bands (*"vague pricing reads as scared"*). The other four said no prices. **Decision: no prices on the page.** Pricing belongs in the conversation that follows.

**Tempo on going public.** Branson + Naval + Godin: one small visible act this month (postcard / artifact / list). Munger + Greene: nothing public until day 90 — no data earned. The page falls in the middle: *exposed but not broadcast* — sent in conversation only. Both camps satisfied.

## Open thread (revisit before ramping)

**Shape 1 pricing model.** Ahmed's voice-dictated input on the engagement-shapes page suggested *hourly billing* for fractional CTO. The GTM doctrine in `strategy.md` explicitly forbids hourly billing (*"Hourly anchors buyer on time, not outcome. Fractional prices as a monthly retainer with bounded scope, not a day quota"*). Naomi flagged this same tension as a pending unresolved item from the 2026-04-24 session. The page itself does not display pricing — so the contradiction is not visible to readers — but the underlying question stands: hourly, daily (au journalier), or monthly retainer with bounded scope? Resolve before the first fractional engagement is signed.

**Shape 4 (training) pricing model.** Not specified. Half-day to two days, per session.

**The "let one of the four die by day 45" data point (Naval).** Pull is data — read it. By mid-June, one of the four shapes will have produced more inbound resonance than the others. Note which without forcing the call.

**Singer's instruction to disconnect for two weeks.** Ahmed's plan: continue the work for two more weeks, then disconnect for two weeks (mid-May → end of May), see what surfaces. Honor it. The architecture's job during the disconnect is to not generate new structures.
