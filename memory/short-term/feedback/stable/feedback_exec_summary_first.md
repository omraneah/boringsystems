**Rule:** Any dense analysis, research output, or multi-section document starts with an Executive Summary of 10–20 bullets maximum. The bullets come first — before any section headers, before any narrative detail, before any tables. No exceptions.

**Why:** Ahmed framed this explicitly: "protect my cognition." A dense document without a summary forces re-reading the whole thing to extract signal — scanning sections for the punchline, re-tracing steps already taken. An upfront summary lets him decide in 30 seconds whether to read on, what to skip, and where to redirect. This is the same discipline applied to the `tmp/` rule (long output off the terminal) — it's the companion rule: where the output goes, and how it's structured.

**How to apply:**

Trigger conditions — apply the rule whenever:
- Writing a `tmp/` document of any kind
- Any multi-section response (3+ sections, regardless of length)
- Any GTM analysis (LinkedIn copy audit, offer framing, market read)
- Any market or positioning research output
- Any structured comparison (options, tradeoffs, stacks, candidates)
- Any response that would take more than 2 minutes to skim in full
- Any advisory board output or decision memo
- Any document with a table of contents or more than one H2 heading

Format of the Executive Summary:
- Header: `## Executive Summary`
- 10–20 bullets, each one a complete signal (not a topic label)
- Bullets are ordered: most important first, context last
- No nested sub-bullets in the summary itself
- After the summary: full detail sections as normal

What counts as a complete signal in a bullet:
- Good: "LinkedIn headline under-performs — opener buries the transformation claim until word 8"
- Bad: "LinkedIn headline analysis" (topic label, not signal)

The summary must stand alone. If someone reads only the summary, they should have the decision-relevant information. Details are for verification, not for discovery.
