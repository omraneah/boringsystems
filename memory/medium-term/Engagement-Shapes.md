# Engagement Shapes — A and B

Two shapes. Not categories of the same thing; structurally distinct surfaces with different time-to-close, risk profiles, sourcing channels, and capture risk. Both serve the same [[Drivers-and-Filters]] position synthesis.

> **Influence-based change leadership in complex orgs rooted in real constraints — engineering, systems, teams, operations — hands-on under half, no direct-report-heavy authority, in France, with people I'm aligned with.**

---

## Shape A — Mandate inside a company

**Structure.** Full-time engagement inside one company. CDI / employment, or equivalent fixed-mandate inside an existing org.

**Sourcing.** LinkedIn / recruiters (inbound). Warm intros into senior-mandate roles.

**Time-to-close.** Longer — weeks to months, depending on org cadence.

**Capture risk.** Higher. A full-time mandate inside one org consolidates exposure on one relationship; if alignment slips, exit cost is material.

**Income.** Stable salary.

**Drivers it lights up.** Serve (when alignment real), curiosity (change in complex orgs at full immersion), alignment with people (lived daily), rootedness in reality (full-scope contact with the operating constraint), serendipity (the right mandate arriving through a closed door).

**Filters that disqualify in this shape.** Hands-on > 50 %, kernel-IC, hustle/prove framing, kill-yourself culture, team-leadership-as-main-mandate (this period), analytics-as-tail-of-business, engineering-as-only-surface.

**Validity test for Shape A.** Per [[Engagement-Validity-Filter]] + [[Path-Doctrine]] necessary conditions — day-to-day texture intact, I-AM aligned, room for own-things to accumulate, real epistemic upside, structural safety (exit rights, no identity foreclosure). If all hold, Shape A is valid. Employment is **not** disqualified by being employment — only by misalignment.

## Shape B — Fractional / Freelance via warm network

**Structure.** Part-time judgment-altitude engagement. Two sub-shapes.

### Sub-shape B.1 — Fractional CTO / CPO, early-stage

- **Cadence:** ≤ 2 days/week. Multi-month commit.
- **Altitude:** judgment-altitude — level practices, hold standards, audit, decide trade-offs, ensure the team can deliver.
- **Posture:** touch-to-enable, not to own. Set scaffolding, coach the existing dev(s), produce decision artifacts that survive Ahmed's exit. Never become the main dev.

### Sub-shape B.2 — Transformation freelance for established orgs

- **Cadence:** scoped, time-boxed, multi-week to multi-month.
- **Altitude:** advisory-heavy. Lead a bounded transformation (migration, platform consolidation, vendor exit, tech-debt unwind) — architect, sequence, coordinate. Not IC delivery.
- **Buyer:** larger companies with in-flight transformations who lack someone at the right altitude to own end-to-end.

### Engagement principles (Shape B, both sub-shapes)

- **Discovery session first.** Default entry point: in-person or video working session, 2-3 hours, no scope pre-decided. Come to where they work. Explore together. Scope emerges from the session, or a clean no.
- **Free input where it serves.** Small probes, max 1-2 sessions, with hard scope limit. No open-ended free work. The act of sharing a portable playbook openly *is* the probe — see contained-risk principle below.
- **Paid threshold.** 100 €/h · 800 €/day. Below this, the engagement is priced as labor, not leverage. The threshold is the floor for paid Shape B work.
- **Clear mandate.** Every Shape B engagement has a named outcome. No rolling retainer without a milestone gate.
- **Contained scope.** Time-boxed. Renewable on explicit re-evaluation, not by default.

### Contained-risk principle (non-negotiable, Shape B)

A fractional CTO / freelance lead carries **contained risk** on what they own: architectural decisions, technical standards, team velocity, scoped delivery.

A fractional CTO / freelance lead does **NOT** carry **founder-level risk**: business outcome, market fit, runway, equity-grade incentive alignment.

Early-stage founders implicitly want the second. That's the shape that gets refused. When a studio or founder proposes incentive alignment ("you'll be invested in the outcome", "we'll share equity once we figure out the structure", "co-direction means you carry the project like a founder"), name the asymmetry out loud:

> External parties cannot carry founder-grade incentives on a project that is not theirs. Pretending otherwise is meant to fail.

The convergence point is not Ahmed's project. He is contributing in a defined way. That is the honest frame.

### Re-audit as exit mechanism (Shape B, studio-routed)

When the studio that introduced the engagement is also the entity that produced the initial audit, the **re-audit at a milestone gate becomes the natural exit mechanism**. The studio re-runs the audit at month-N against the foundations work delivered. If the delta is material, the conversation continues. If not, the studio (not Ahmed) tells the founder the foundations aren't ready for the next phase — and Ahmed exits clean without being the bad-news messenger. Build this in from the start.

### Drivers Shape B lights up

Serve (when the encounter arrives clean), curiosity (change in complex orgs at the judgment layer), freedom (light cadence preserves the day), alignment with people (chosen per engagement, not assigned), rootedness in reality (in the operating constraint without being captured by it).

### Filters that disqualify in Shape B

Founder-level risk ownership, hands-on > 50 %, becoming the main dev, infrastructure-tooling-knowledge captured in Ahmed's head, kill-yourself culture, hustle/prove framing.

## Comparison

| Dimension | Shape A | Shape B |
|---|---|---|
| Cadence | Full-time | ≤ 2 days/week (B.1) · scoped time-box (B.2) |
| Count | 1 mandate | Possibly multiple (light) or one at a time |
| Commitment | Open-ended (CDI) or fixed-term | Multi-month with milestone gates |
| Risk owned | Salary-employee scope | Contained on what's delivered; never founder-grade |
| Capture risk | Higher (single relationship) | Lower (multiple relationships, time-boxed) |
| Time-to-close | Weeks to months | Days to weeks |
| Income | Stable salary | Per-engagement, fee-based |
| Sourcing | LinkedIn / recruiters / warm intros to senior mandates | Warm network primarily; studio/agency routing secondarily |

Both shapes are valid. The shape that satisfies the [[Drivers-and-Filters]] AND [[Engagement-Validity-Filter]] AND [[Path-Doctrine]] necessary conditions wins. Ideology about preferred shape doesn't.

## Closing non-fits cleanly

When a scope is clearly misaligned (e.g., pure execution in an unfamiliar stack, founder-grade incentive request on a Shape B engagement), close it honestly and early:

> "I operate at the intersection of multiple domains — that's where my leverage shows up. This scope is more pure execution; you'd get more value from a senior dev."

> "External parties can't carry founder-grade incentives on a project that's not theirs. The shape you're describing is co-founder, not fractional."

Don't mention pricing as the reason. The altitude or risk-shape mismatch is the reason.

---

## The "yes" signal

A potential engagement (either shape) should feel **lighter** after exploring it, not heavier. If a scoping conversation produces clarity and some energy, continue. If it produces obligation and weight, decline — regardless of logic or money. See [[current-arc]] § Navigation compass.
