# Pre-Funnel Aesthetic Filter

**Rule:** Filter on register before the funnel. Filter on thesis inside the funnel.

## The split

**Pre-funnel (cold stranger — LinkedIn header, conference bio, podcast title, any first-contact surface):**
The aesthetic register is the filter. The reader decides "is this person in my world?" before reading a word of the argument. If the register reads wrong — too soft, too marketing-flavored, too generic — the right people self-select out before the thesis has a chance.

**Inside the funnel (site, article, DM, proposal — second-click and deeper):**
Register is already confirmed. The filter shifts to thesis: is the argument sharp enough, specific enough, true enough? Polish and voice carry less weight; substance carries everything.

## Consequence

- LinkedIn header / podcast guest spot / conference bio: optimize for register signal, not thesis completeness. The goal is resonance with the right reader, not coverage.
- boringsystems homepage: umbrella register (operator-to-operator, editorial, constraint-first) — designed so the right reader feels at home, wrong reader self-selects out.
- Case files / articles: thesis-first. Register is assumed; argument is the filter.

## Why this isn't obvious

The instinct is to front-load the argument at every touchpoint ("here's what I do, here's why it matters"). This works inside the funnel. Pre-funnel, it reads as a pitch — and pitches leak register. The filter must happen at the register layer first or the right audience never enters.

## Application

Any new cold-stranger touchpoint: ask "is this pre-funnel or inside-funnel?" before writing copy. Pre-funnel → optimize register. Inside-funnel → optimize argument.

When evaluating existing copy for a new channel: same question first.
