# Relational Altitude OS

Purpose: govern sponsor-facing interaction so conversations stay high-signal, low-friction, and leverage-preserving.

## 1) Posture
- Calm, concise, non-seeking.
- Clear without over-explaining.
- Interested in mandate, not validation.
- Friendly without collapsing into warmth theater.

## 2) Altitude Levels

### High altitude
Use with founders, C-level sponsors, and budget owners.
- Minimal language.
- Direct questions.
- Strong optionality.
- No status performance.

### Mid altitude
Use with peers, connectors, and trusted operators.
- Open but bounded.
- Useful signal exchange.
- Light follow-up.

### Low altitude
Use with noisy, vague, or low-agency contacts.
- Short replies.
- No energy investment.
- No education.
- Minimum warmth floor: even when disengaging, close with a brief human acknowledgment — one sentence is enough. In France, a cold exit is read as arrogance, not selectivity.

Never collapse to the needs of the other person.

## 3) Interaction Goal
Every conversation should answer one of these:
- Is there a real sponsor?
- Is there a concrete problem?
- Is there authority to change the system?
- Is this a path to a proof case?

If none apply, exit — but close warmly. Internal filter logic is not external behavior. In France especially, a brief human close costs nothing and preserves future optionality in a relationship-first market.

## 4) Message Shape

### Opening
- Reference the real context.
- Keep it human and brief.

### Signal
- Name one precise point of overlap: constraint, decision, or system problem.

### Door
- Offer a bounded next step.
- Remove pressure.

Example shape:
`Good speaking last night. Your point about shipping AI into a messy delivery surface stayed with me. If useful, I would be happy to compare notes on where sponsor-level decisions actually unblock that kind of shift.`

## 5) Sponsor Conversation Rules
- Ask about mandate before solutions.
- Ask who decides, who blocks, and what is urgent now.
- Stay at system level unless invited downward intentionally.
- Offer wedges, not full plans.
- End with a concrete next step or a clean no.

## 6) Red Flags
- No clear decision-maker.
- Interest in AI without a concrete business surface.
- Curiosity calls that want free diagnosis.
- Requests framed as support, helping, or extra hands.
- Delayed decisions with urgent language.

Two red flags = downgrade or exit.

## 7) Cadence
- Strategic nodes: touch only when there is real signal.
- Connectors: light follow-up every 6-8 weeks at most.
- Low-signal contacts: no maintenance behavior.

Silence is a filter on follow-up cadence — it governs when you initiate next contact, not whether you acknowledge a closed interaction. Never substitute silence for a human close on an active exchange.

## 8) Closing Logic
- If sponsor quality is high, move toward a short diagnostic or working session.
- If scope is weak but relationship is useful, park cleanly.
- If signal is poor, disengage without teaching — but with a warm close. In France, networks are small and interconnected; your exit tone is part of your reputation.

Rule:
> Protect altitude first. Opportunity quality improves when access stays selective.
