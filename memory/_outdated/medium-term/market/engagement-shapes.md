# Engagement Shapes & Pricing

## The value shape

End-to-end ownership of a complex area where a proven playbook already exists. Short time, high leverage. Not generalist advisory, not senior execution. Operates at intersections — product, growth, system — and unlocks value through engineering, but not only through engineering.

The leverage is most apparent when: the scope is complex enough to require cross-domain judgment, the outcome is defined, and execution can happen fast because the pattern has been seen before.

## Pricing

**Outcome-based, not hourly.** Hourly creates micro-tracking friction on both sides. Price the outcome.

- **~€5k** — defined scope, bounded time (~half-time or less over one month)
- **~€10k** — near full-time month, larger scope

Internal sanity check: does the outcome price roughly match €100/hr minimum? That's the floor. Never surface this math to the client.

**Monthly retainer variant:** "Up to X days/week" — priced at the ceiling. If engagement goes deeper because the work is genuinely interesting, that's Ahmed's choice, invisible to the client.

**Free:** only for small, high-curiosity probes. Max 1-2 sessions. Hard scope limit stated upfront. No open-ended commitment — if it drifts, close it.

**No half-price-with-caveats.** Discounted + "I might leave" = anti-selling. Either full rate or free. The middle tier dissolves with clear anchors.

## Exploration before commitment

Default entry point with potential collaborators: **in-person working session, 2-3 hours, no scope pre-decided.**

- Come to where they work
- Explore different areas together
- See what comes out — real pain points, fit signal, collaboration chemistry
- If it doesn't feel right → clear no
- If it does → scope emerges naturally from the session

This is better than a discovery call (too formal, puts Ahmed in sales mode) and better than jumping to a scoped proposal (premature).

## Closing non-fits cleanly

When a scope is clearly misaligned (e.g., pure senior execution in an unfamiliar stack), close it honestly and early:

> "I operate at the intersection of multiple domains — that's where my leverage appears. This scope is more pure execution; you'd get more value from a senior dev."

Don't mention pricing as the reason. The altitude mismatch is the reason.

## The signal for "yes"

A potential collaboration should feel lighter after discussing it, not heavier. If exploring a scope produces clarity and some energy, continue. If it produces obligation and weight, decline — regardless of the logic or the money.
